1. Indian Polity: Fundamental Rights, DPSPs & Important Articles

The Constitution of India, adopted on 26 November 1949 and effective from 26 January 1950, is the supreme legal framework of the republic. For competitive exams, understanding Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles of State Policy, and vital constitutional articles is essential for scoring full marks.

Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35)

Known as the Magna Carta of India, Fundamental Rights are justiciable in courts of law. Originally, seven Fundamental Rights were guaranteed, but the Right to Property (Article 31) was deleted by the 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978 and made a legal right under Article 300A (Part XII). Currently, there are six fundamental rights:

Category Articles Covered Constitutional Scope
Right to Equality Articles 14–18 Equality before law (Art 14), prohibition of discrimination (Art 15), equality of opportunity in public employment (Art 16), abolition of untouchability (Art 17), and abolition of titles (Art 18).
Right to Freedom Articles 19–22 Six democratic freedoms under Article 19, protection in conviction (Art 20), protection of life and personal liberty (Art 21), right to elementary education (Art 21A), and protection against arbitrary arrest (Art 22).
Right against Exploitation Articles 23–24 Prohibition of human trafficking and forced labour (Art 23), and prohibition of employment of children below 14 years in factories and hazardous work (Art 24).
Right to Freedom of Religion Articles 25–28 Freedom of conscience and free profession (Art 25), management of religious affairs (Art 26), freedom from tax promotion of religion (Art 27), and attendance at religious worship in state-aided institutions (Art 28).
Cultural & Educational Rights Articles 29–30 Protection of language, script, and culture of minorities (Art 29), and right of minorities to establish and administer educational institutions (Art 30).
Right to Constitutional Remedies Article 32 Right to move the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar termed Article 32 the "Heart and Soul of the Constitution". Supreme Court (Art 32) and High Courts (Art 226) issue five types of writs: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, and Quo-Warranto.

Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Articles 36–51)

Borrowed from the Irish Constitution (which in turn borrowed them from Spain), DPSPs embody the concept of a "Welfare State". Under Article 37, DPSPs are non-justiciable (courts cannot legally compel the government to execute them), yet they are fundamental in the governance of the country. They are broadly categorized into three ideologies:

  • Socialistic Principles: Article 38 (promoting public welfare), Article 39 (equitable distribution of resources, prevention of concentration of wealth), Article 39A (equal justice and free legal aid), Article 41 (right to work and education), and Article 43 (living wage for workers).
  • Gandhian Principles: Article 40 (organisation of Village Panchayats), Article 43 (promotion of cottage industries), Article 46 (promotion of educational and economic interests of SCs, STs, and weaker sections), Article 47 (prohibition of intoxicating drinks and drugs), and Article 48 (prohibition of slaughter of cows, calves, and milch cattle).
  • Liberal-Intellectual Principles: Article 44 (Uniform Civil Code for all citizens throughout India), Article 45 (early childhood care and education below 6 years), Article 48A (protection of environment, forests, and wildlife), Article 49 (protection of monuments and places of national importance), Article 50 (separation of judiciary from executive), and Article 51 (promotion of international peace and security).

📌 Key points to remember
  • Fundamental Rights under Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended even during a National Emergency (Article 359 amended by 44th Amendment Act, 1978).
  • Articles 15, 16, 19, 29, and 30 are available only to Indian citizens, whereas Articles 14, 20, 21, 21A, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, and 28 are available to both citizens and foreigners (except enemy aliens).
  • Financial Emergency under Article 360 has never been declared in India till date.
  • Article 32 gives the Supreme Court original writ jurisdiction, whereas High Courts derive wider writ jurisdiction (for both Fundamental Rights and legal rights) under Article 226.

2. Geography: Major Rivers, National Parks, Soil Types & Mountain Ranges

Indian physical geography covers diverse drainage networks, unique bio-reserves, distinctive pedological zones, and magnificent orographic barriers. Here are the core verifiable static facts tested in competitive examinations.

2. Notable National Parks & Biosphere Reserves

India hosts over 106 National Parks under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972. Examiners frequently target record-holding and biodiversity-rich sanctuaries:

  • Jim Corbett National Park (Uttarakhand): Established in 1936 as Hailey National Park; India's first national park and the launch site of Project Tiger (1973).
  • Hemis National Park (Ladakh): The largest national park in India (approx. 4,400 sq km); situated at high altitude and famous for snow leopards.
  • South Button Island National Park (Andaman & Nicobar Islands): The smallest national park in India (~0.03 sq km).
  • Kaziranga National Park (Assam): UNESCO World Heritage Site; home to world's highest population of the Great Indian One-horned Rhinoceros.
  • Keibul Lamjao National Park (Manipur): Located on Loktak Lake; the world's only floating national park, famous for the endangered Sangai (brow-antlered deer) on floating biomass called phumdis.
  • Gir National Park (Gujarat): The exclusive and sole natural habitat of the Asiatic Lion (Panthera leo persica) in the world.

3. Major Soil Types of India (ICAR Classification)

Soil Group Geographical Spread & Characteristics Major Crops
Alluvial Soil Covers ~40% of India's landmass (Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra plains, coastal deltas). Divided into older alluvium (Bhangar, contains calcareous kankar) and newer alluvium (Khadar, very fertile). Rich in potash and lime; deficient in nitrogen and phosphorus. Wheat, Rice, Sugarcane, Jute, Oilseeds
Black Soil (Regur) Formed by weathering of basaltic lava in the Deccan Trap (Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh). High clay content, self-ploughing nature (develops wide cracks during summer), and extraordinary moisture retention capacity. Rich in iron, lime, calcium, magnesium; poor in nitrogen, phosphorus, and organic matter. Cotton (frequently termed Black Cotton Soil), Soybean, Tobacco, Millets
Red & Yellow Soil Formed on crystalline igneous rocks in low rainfall areas of Deccan plateau, Odisha, and Chhattisgarh. Red color is due to the diffusion of iron oxides; appears yellow when hydrated. Deficient in nitrogen, phosphorus, and humus. Pulses, Millets, Groundnut, Tobacco
Laterite Soil Formed under conditions of high temperature and alternate intense wet and dry spells with heavy leaching (Western Ghats, Meghalaya plateau). Rich in bauxite, iron, and aluminium oxides; poor in nitrogen, potassium, and organic matter. Tea, Coffee, Cashew nuts, Rubber

4. Mountain Ranges of India

India's physiography features both young tertiary fold mountains and ancient relict fold ranges:

  • The Himalayas: Young fold mountains formed by collision of Indian and Eurasian plates. Divided longitudinal zones include:
    • Himadri (Greater Himalayas): Average elevation ~6,000 m; contains Mount Everest (8,848.86 m) and Kanchenjunga (8,586 m, highest peak in India's undisputed territory).
    • Himachal (Lesser Himalayas): Elevation 3,700–4,500 m; famous for hill stations like Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital, and Pir Panjal range.
    • Shiwaliks (Outer Himalayas): Youngest zone, elevation 900–1,100 m; characterized by flat-bottom longitudinal valleys known as Duns (e.g., Dehradun).
  • Trans-Himalayas & Karakoram Range: Houses Mount K2 (Godwin-Austen, 8,611 m), the 2nd highest peak in the world (in Pak-occupied Kashmir) and the Siachen Glacier (longest glacier in India).
  • Aravalli Range: One of the oldest fold mountain systems in the world, running across Gujarat, Rajasthan, Haryana, and Delhi. Highest peak is Guru Shikhar (1,722 m) on Mount Abu.
  • Western Ghats (Sahyadri): Continuous mountain chain along the western coast recognized as a global biodiversity hotspot. Highest peak is Anamudi (2,695 m) in the Anaimalai Hills (Kerala), which is also the highest peak in Peninsular India.
  • Eastern Ghats: Discontinuous, eroded range dissected by major east-flowing rivers. Highest peak is Jindhagada Peak (1,690 m) in Andhra Pradesh. Western and Eastern Ghats meet at the Nilgiri Hills (highest peak: Doddabetta, 2,637 m).
📌 Key points to remember
  • Majuli on the Brahmaputra in Assam is officially the world's largest river island certified by Guinness World Records.
  • Narmada and Tapti flow from east to west in rift valleys and do not form deltas; they discharge into the Gulf of Khambhat (Arabian Sea).
  • The Tropic of Cancer (23.5° N) passes through 8 Indian states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram.
  • Anamudi (Kerala) is the highest mountain peak in all of Peninsular India, situated south of the Palghat gap.

3. Modern History: Chronological Timeline from 1857 to 1947

The freedom struggle of India is the cornerstone of modern Indian history in competitive examinations. This verified chronological timeline captures every pivotal event, charter, movement, and constitutional turning point from the Great Revolt of 1857 to Independence in 1947.

1857
The Revolt of 1857 (First War of Indian Independence)

Began at Meerut on May 10, 1857, following Mangal Pandey's revolt at Barrackpore on March 29. Bahadur Shah Zafar was proclaimed Shahenshah-e-Hindustan. Lord Canning was the Governor-General during the revolt.

1858
Government of India Act 1858 & Queen’s Proclamation

East India Company rule was abolished; British Crown assumed direct administration. The Governor-General was designated Viceroy of India (Lord Canning became the 1st Viceroy).

1885
Foundation of the Indian National Congress (INC)

Founded by retired British civil servant Allan Octavian (A.O.) Hume. The 1st session took place at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay, presided over by Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee with 72 delegates. Lord Dufferin was Viceroy.

1905
Partition of Bengal & Swadeshi Movement

Announced by Lord Curzon on July 20 and effective October 16, 1905. Led to the historic Swadeshi and Boycott Movement launched at the Calcutta Town Hall on August 7, 1905.

1906
Formation of the All-India Muslim League

Founded at Dacca (Dhaka) under the leadership of Nawab Salimullah, Aga Khan, and Mohsin-ul-Mulk to protect Muslim political interests.

1907
Surat Split of the Congress

INC split into Moderates (led by Gopal Krishna Gokhale) and Extremists (led by Lal-Bal-Pal). The Surat session was presided over by Rash Behari Ghosh.

1909
Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909)

Introduced separate electorates for Muslims, introducing communal representation. Lord Minto is known as the "Father of Communal Electorate".

1911
Delhi Durbar: Capital Shifted & Partition Annulled

King George V visited India; Partition of Bengal was annulled. The capital of British India was officially shifted from Calcutta to Delhi.

1915
Mahatma Gandhi Returns to India

Mahatma Gandhi returned from South Africa on January 9, 1915 (commemorated as Pravasi Bharatiya Divas). Gopal Krishna Gokhale was his political mentor.

1916
Lucknow Pact & Home Rule League Movement

INC and Muslim League signed the Lucknow Pact (presided by A.C. Majumdar). Moderates and Extremists reunited. Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Annie Besant launched independent Home Rule Leagues.

1917–1918
Early Gandhian Satyagrahas: Champaran, Ahmedabad & Kheda

1917: Champaran Satyagraha in Bihar against the Tinkathia system of indigo cultivation (Gandhi's first Civil Disobedience in India). 1918: Ahmedabad Mill Strike (first hunger strike) & Kheda Satyagraha (first non-cooperation).

1919
Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh Massacre & Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms

Rowlatt Act authorized detention without trial. On April 13, 1919, General Dyer ordered troops to fire on an unarmed peaceful gathering at Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar. Rabindranath Tagore renounced his Knighthood. Government of India Act 1919 introduced Dyarchy in provinces.

1920–1922
Non-Cooperation Movement & Khilafat Movement

Launched on August 1, 1920 (the day Bal Gangadhar Tilak passed away). Gandhi surrendered his Kaiser-i-Hind medal. On February 4, 1922, violent clashes at Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur, UP) caused Gandhi to suspend the movement abruptly on February 12, 1922.

1927–1928
Simon Commission & Nehru Report

All-white Indian Statutory Commission (Simon Commission) appointed in 1927; arrived in Bombay in February 1928 facing nationwide "Simon Go Back" protests. Lala Lajpat Rai succumbed to lathi-charge injuries in Lahore (October 1928). All-Parties Conference produced Motilal Nehru Report (1928) demanding Dominion Status.

1929
Lahore Session of the INC: Historic Purna Swaraj Declaration

Presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru on the banks of River Ravi. Congress declared Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence) as its ultimate goal and decided to celebrate January 26, 1930 as Independence Day.

1930–1934
Dandi March & Civil Disobedience Movement

On March 12, 1930, Gandhi marched from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi with 78 chosen followers (240 miles) and broke the Salt Law on April 6, 1930. The movement was paused by the Gandhi-Irwin Pact (March 5, 1931), enabling Gandhi to attend the 2nd Round Table Conference in London.

1932
Communal Award & Poona Pact

British PM Ramsay MacDonald announced the Communal Award. Gandhi undertook a fast-unto-death in Yerwada Jail (Pune). The Poona Pact was signed on September 24, 1932 between Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and Madan Mohan Malaviya (on behalf of caste Hindus), abandoning separate electorates in favor of reserved seats for Depressed Classes.

1935
Government of India Act 1935

Abolished Dyarchy in provinces and introduced Provincial Autonomy. Proposed an All-India Federation and Dyarchy at the Centre. Established the Federal Court (1937) and Reserve Bank of India (1935).

1942
Cripps Mission & The Quit India Movement

Stafford Cripps proposed post-war Dominion status, rejected by Gandhi as a "post-dated cheque on a crashing bank". On August 8, 1942, INC passed the historic Quit India Resolution at Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay. Gandhi gave the famous clarion call: "Do or Die".

1946
Cabinet Mission Plan & Constituent Assembly

Three-member British Cabinet Mission (Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander) visited India. It rejected a separate sovereign Pakistan and proposed a constituent body. The Constituent Assembly held its first meeting on December 9, 1946 with Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha as temporary President.

1947
Mountbatten Plan & Indian Independence Act 1947

Lord Mountbatten announced the 3rd June Plan for partition. The British Parliament passed the Indian Independence Act on July 18, 1947. At midnight of August 14–15, 1947, India gained independence, ending 190 years of British colonial rule.

📌 Key points to remember
  • Mahatma Gandhi presided over only one session of the Indian National Congress: the Belgaum Session of 1924.
  • Subhash Chandra Bose was elected Congress President twice: Haripura (1938) and Tripuri (1939). After resigning due to differences with Gandhi, he formed the Forward Bloc in 1939.
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and Tej Bahadur Sapru attended all three Round Table Conferences (1930, 1931, and 1932) held in London.
  • Sarojini Naidu was the first Indian woman to become Congress President (Kanpur Session, 1925), whereas Annie Besant was the first woman overall (Calcutta Session, 1917).

4. Memory Tricks: 5 High-Yield Mnemonics with Examples

Mnemonics drastically reduce cognitive load and eliminate negative marking during objective competitive exams. Here are five verified memory acronyms tested repeatedly across exams.

Polity Mnemonic
PRASAD → 5 Types of Writs (Art 32 & 226) / "HCPM-Q"

Mnemonic Formula: H - C - P - M - Q

Expansion:

  • H – Habeas Corpus (To have the body of; protects against illegal detention).
  • C – Certiorari (To be certified or informed; quashes order of a lower court exceeding jurisdiction).
  • P – Prohibition (To forbid; issued to an inferior court to prevent exceeding its jurisdiction during trial).
  • M – Mandamus (We command; compels a public official or body to perform a mandatory statutory duty).
  • Q – Quo-Warranto (By what authority; inquires into the legality of a person holding a public office).

Example in Exam: When asked "Which writ is issued against a private individual?", remember that only Habeas Corpus can be issued against both public authorities and private individuals.

Geography Mnemonic
GRB MT JMT → 8 States on Tropic of Cancer (23.5° N)

Mnemonic Sentence: "Gujarat Rajasthan Bath, MP Chhattisgarh Jhar, West Tripura Mizo" or simple sequence "G - R - M - C - J - W - T - M"

West-to-East Geographic Order:

  1. G – Gujarat
  2. R – Rajasthan (passes through Banswara)
  3. M – Madhya Pradesh (longest stretch)
  4. C – Chhattisgarh
  5. J – Jharkhand (passes close to Ranchi)
  6. W – West Bengal
  7. T – Tripura
  8. M – Mizoram

Example in Exam: SSC frequently asks: "Which state does NOT lie on the Tropic of Cancer: Odisha, Jharkhand, Gujarat, or Rajasthan?" Using the mnemonic, Odisha is instantly eliminated.

Medieval / Modern History Mnemonic
BHAJSA → Chronology of 6 Great Mughal Emperors

Mnemonic Formula: B - H - A - J - S - A

Expansion:

  • B – Babur (1526–1530): Won 1st Battle of Panipat (1526) against Ibrahim Lodi.
  • H – Humayun (1530–1540 & 1555–1556): Defeated by Sher Shah Suri at Kannauj.
  • A – Akbar (1556–1605): Won 2nd Battle of Panipat (1556); introduced Mansabdari system.
  • J – Jahangir (1605–1627): Executed 5th Sikh Guru Arjan Dev; Captain William Hawkins visited his court.
  • S – Shah Jahan (1628–1658): Golden Age of Mughal Architecture (built Taj Mahal, Red Fort).
  • A – Aurangzeb (1658–1707): Assumed title Alamgir (World Conqueror); reimposed Jizya (1679).

Example in Exam: Match the following dynasties or chronological order: easily recall who succeeded whom without mixing Jahangir and Shah Jahan.

International GK Mnemonic
MBBS PAIN → 8 Member Nations of SAARC

Mnemonic Formula: M - B - B - S - P - A - I - N

Expansion:

  • M – Maldives
  • B – Bangladesh (SAARC founded in Dhaka, 1985)
  • B – Bhutan
  • S – Sri Lanka
  • P – Pakistan
  • A – Afghanistan (joined as 8th member in 2007)
  • I – India
  • N – Nepal (SAARC Secretariat situated in Kathmandu)

Example in Exam: Questions frequently tempt candidates with Myanmar or Mauritius. By remembering "MBBS PAIN", you know 'M' stands for Maldives, NOT Myanmar!

Polity Mnemonic
SPAM-RO → Article 19(1) Six Fundamental Freedoms

Mnemonic Phrase: "Speech Assembly Association Movement Residence Occupation"

Constitutional Clauses under Article 19(1):

  • 19(1)(a): Freedom of Speech and expression.
  • 19(1)(b): Freedom to assemble Peaceably without arms.
  • 19(1)(c): Freedom to form Associations, unions, or co-operative societies.
  • 19(1)(d): Freedom to Move freely throughout the territory of India.
  • 19(1)(e): Freedom to Reside and settle in any part of India.
  • [Article 19(1)(f) Property deleted by 44th Amendment]
  • 19(1)(g): Freedom to practise any profession, or carry on any Occupation, trade, or business.

Example in Exam: Identify which sub-clause covers the right to form cooperative societies — immediately identify 19(1)(c).

📌 Key points to remember
  • In SAARC, Myanmar and China are not members; they are only observer nations.
  • The Tropic of Cancer does not cut through Odisha; it passes strictly north of it through Jharkhand and West Bengal.
  • Right to strike is not a Fundamental Right under Article 19(1)(a) or 19(1)(c).
  • The writ of Quo-Warranto cannot be sought against private bodies or ministerial posts; it only applies to substantive public offices created by statute or constitution.

5. Practice: 10 High-Yield Exam Questions with Hidden Solutions

Assess your retention with these 10 exam-level multiple choice questions. After solving each question, click "Show Answer & Explanation" to reveal the detailed verified solution.

Q1 Which Article of the Constitution of India provides for the abolition of Untouchability and forbids its practice in any form?
Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: Article 17

Article 17 explicitly declares that "Untouchability is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden." Parliament enacted the Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955, later amended and renamed the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955. Unlike several other Fundamental Rights, Article 17 is absolute and available against private individuals as well.

Q2 Which is the only National Park in the world that is a floating wildlife sanctuary?
Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: Keibul Lamjao National Park (Manipur)

Located on the freshwater Loktak Lake in the Bishnupur district of Manipur, Keibul Lamjao is the world's only floating national park. It is characterized by floating decomposed vegetative matter known locally as phumdis, and is the last natural refuge of the endangered Sangai or dancing deer (Rucervus eldii eldii).

Q3 In which session of the Indian National Congress was the resolution of 'Purna Swaraj' (Complete Independence) formally adopted?
Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: Lahore Session, 1929

The Lahore Session was held in December 1929 under the presidency of Jawaharlal Nehru. The Congress declared 'Purna Swaraj' instead of Dominion Status as its primary objective. The tricolour was unfurled on midnight of December 31, 1929 along the banks of River Ravi, and January 26, 1930 was fixed as the First Independence Day.

Q4 Which writ is issued by the Supreme Court or High Courts to compel a public official to perform a duty that they have failed or refused to execute?
Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: Mandamus

Mandamus literally translates to "We Command". It is a judicial command issued to a public official, tribunal, or lower court directing them to perform a mandatory statutory duty incumbent upon their office. It cannot be issued against the President of India or State Governors for the exercise of their official powers.

Q5 What is the highest mountain peak in Peninsular India?
Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: Anamudi (2,695 metres)

Anamudi is located in the Idukki district of Kerala within the Eravikulam National Park. It is situated on the Anaimalai Hills of the Western Ghats and stands as the highest elevation peak in India south of the Himalayas. Doddabetta (2,637 m) in the Nilgiris is the second highest.

Q6 Under which Constitutional Amendment Act was the Right to Education inserted as Article 21A into the Indian Constitution?
Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002

The 86th Amendment Act, 2002 made education a Fundamental Right under Article 21A, guaranteeing free and compulsory education to all children aged 6 to 14 years. It also amended Article 45 (in DPSPs) and added the 11th Fundamental Duty under Article 51A(k). Parliament subsequently passed the Right to Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act in 2009.

Q7 Which soil type is known for its remarkable self-ploughing capacity and wide cracks that develop during dry weather?
Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: Black Soil (Regur Soil)

Black soil is clayey and contains high levels of montmorillonite clay minerals. It swells and becomes sticky when wet, and shrinks drastically upon drying, developing deep wide cracks. This process allows natural aeration and is termed "self-ploughing". It is ideal for dry-farming and rainfed cotton cultivation.

Q8 Who among the following was the only person to attend all three Round Table Conferences held in London between 1930 and 1932?
Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (along with Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru)

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar represented the Depressed Classes in all three Round Table Conferences (1930, 1931, and 1932). Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru also participated in all three. Mahatma Gandhi represented the Indian National Congress only at the Second Round Table Conference in 1931 after signing the Gandhi-Irwin Pact.

Q9 Which two major peninsular rivers of India flow westwards through rift valleys and drain into the Arabian Sea without creating deltas?
Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: Narmada and Tapti (Tapi)

The Narmada (originating from Amarkantak) and Tapti (originating from Multai in Betul, MP) flow westward in structural trough/rift valleys created by faulting. Because of high flow velocity through narrow rocky beds, they deposit their sediment directly into deep coastal waters, forming linear estuaries rather than fan-shaped deltas.

Q10 The historic Poona Pact of September 1932 was signed between Mahatma Gandhi and which prominent leader?
Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Signed on September 24, 1932 at Yerwada Central Jail in Pune, the Poona Pact was an agreement between Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (representing the Depressed Classes) and Madan Mohan Malaviya with other Hindu leaders. It abandoned British Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald's Communal Award for separate electorates and instead increased reserved seats for depressed classes in provincial legislatures from 71 to 147.
